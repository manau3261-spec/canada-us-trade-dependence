# Design C · Destination concentration and subsequent export instability

**Status.** Recommended primary design. A within-panel predictive association that can be specified and audited with the validated annual data. No coefficients have been estimated.

1. **Research question.** Among provinces and comparable product groups, does a more concentrated non-U.S. destination portfolio predict greater subsequent instability in the value of exports outside the United States?
2. **Mechanism.** Where destination shocks are weakly correlated, spreading sales across buyers’ markets can dampen movements in total demand. A concentrated portfolio can instead be stable when large destinations have stable demand or when reallocation is costly. Commodity prices and large shipments can shift nominal export value and destination shares together.
3. **Hypothesis.** A higher lagged non-U.S. destination HHI is associated with a larger subsequent absolute annual log change in non-U.S. export value after the fixed effects and predetermined controls. The directional hypothesis is positive, with two-sided inference. A null result is compatible with common destination shocks or offsetting mechanisms.
4. **Dependent variable.** `Y[g,k,t] = |log X[g,k,t+1] − log X[g,k,t]|`, where `X` is non-U.S. domestic merchandise exports in current CAD. This is the magnitude of a one-year change, a proxy for realized instability. It reflects growth spurts as well as contractions and contains price, exchange-rate and quantity movements. Add signed growth and decline-only outcomes for interpretation.
5. **Main explanatory variable.** Strict partner-reconciled `HHI_exUS[g,k,t−1]` at province–product grain on the 0–1 scale. Report the projected difference in absolute log change per 0.10 HHI increment. A concentration measured at `t−1` shares no exact period with the two outcome endpoints `t` and `t+1`. The gap limits direct arithmetic coupling and leaves persistent shocks, anticipation and product composition in play. [HHI reconciliation](hhi_reconciliation.md)
6. **Controls.** Lagged log non-U.S. export level and lagged U.S. share for market size and prior exposure. Both controls use `t−1`. Fixed effects absorb product shocks common to provinces and province-year shocks common to products. Do not include contemporaneous counts, shares or trade values that are components of the outcome in the main model.
7. **Unit.** Province × separately defined NAPCS merchandise section × outcome start year. Use ten provinces and eleven broad sections, omitting total merchandise (a sum) and special transactions (C23). National and territorial results remain in separate descriptive summaries. The NAPCS groups classify merchandise, and the estimand does not assume they are producing industries.
8. **Window.** Annual source years 2010–2025, with 14 start years 2011–2024. In 2024 the predictor is dated 2023 and the outcome compares 2025 with 2024. For `t=2021` the predictor predates the outcome at 2020, where the national aggregate’s residual issue does not automatically mark all product and province rows invalid. Apply cell-level validation. [Phase 3 reconciliation](reconciliation_2020.md)
9. **Sources.** Statistics Canada 12-10-0173-01, customs basis, domestic exports, annual partner and world lines at identical geography/product grain. Table 12-10-0129-01 supplies an official merchandise aggregate HHI benchmark at its available grain. CIMT supplies HS sensitivities in 2024–2025 only in the verified archive. A longer gold-specific sensitivity requires full vintage-matched HS coverage and a concordance that reconstructs the relevant product universe. [Annual bilateral trade](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1210017301) · [Published concentration](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1210012901) · [Exact coverage audit](../data/phase4a/design_C_coverage.csv)
10. **Equation.** `Y[g,k,t] = a[g,k] + b[k,t] + c[g,t] + beta * HHI_exUS[g,k,t−1] + gamma1 * log X[g,k,t−1] + gamma2 * USShare[g,k,t−1] + u[g,k,t]`. Use equal weight per eligible cell in the primary linear projection. Pre-period value weighting is a secondary estimand with greater weight on large portfolios.
11. **Fixed effects.** Province–product `a` for persistent differences, product–year `b` for common category movements and province–year `c` for local aggregate movements. Confirm residual HHI variation, rank and influence before fitting a model. Calendar-year factors refer to the outcome start year.
12. **Inference.** Use province-clustered errors for province-wide serial and cross-product dependence, and studentized restricted wild-cluster bootstrap with Webb weights given ten provinces. Adjacent annual outcomes share one endpoint and cannot be treated as independent. Report leave-one-province-out influence and two-way province and product clustering as sensitivity. Both cluster counts are small. Treat wide or unstable intervals as inconclusive and designate one primary hypothesis before opening results. [Few-cluster inference, Webb](https://doi.org/10.1111/caje.12661)
13. **Identification assumptions.** A stable conditional association and meaningful comparable measurements across cells. A prediction claim requires genuine forward validation using an unconditioned future period or a forecasting baseline whose fixed effects can be known or formed out of sample. A causal effect of a portfolio shift requires exogenous concentration variation independent of expected demand, prices, logistics and selection, which this design does not establish.
14. **Confounding.** Reverse causality from expected instability to search for new markets; firm entry, exit and market-specific growth; persistent prices or exchange rates; high-value commodity accounting; conditioning on continued exporting; shared destination demand; omitted exporter and infrastructure changes. Common fixed effects do not remove local exposure to price shocks or the economic differences between portfolios with identical HHI.
15. **Sample and missingness.** Audit gives **1,540** possible cells and **1,513** with available lagged HHI and positive non-U.S. exports at both endpoints. The 27 failed eligibility checks fall in C12 (24) and C13 (3). A Prince Edward Island C12 singleton leaves at most **1,512** rows across 109 province–product groups for a within-cell fit, before full-rank and all-regressor checks. Report zero and incomplete flows separately. Keep an extensive-margin sensitivity to show whether survival selection masks portfolio exits. Do not turn unresolved values into zeros. [Coverage by cell](../data/phase4a/design_C_coverage.csv)
16. **Robustness.** Pre-specify a small-portfolio threshold and an agreed balanced-panel comparison; compare results with and without NAPCS C14, the metal and non-metallic mineral products section that includes unwrought gold, and with individual products or provinces omitted. Compare largest destination share, top-three shares, number of positive destinations, all-destination HHI and a fixed partner taxonomy. Examine upward vs downward changes, historical endpoints before 2025, and a multi-year non-overlapping outcome to check persistence. Use separate HS quantities only where the unit and reporting universe remain valid. Report how including genuine zero flows in an extensive-margin outcome changes the evaluated population.
17. **Interpretation.** A coefficient would quantify a conditional historical association for active, observed province–product non-U.S. export portfolios. It would support a comparison of observed vulnerability measures. It would not estimate the effect of a policy that forces diversification, a real-volume risk reduction, or welfare gains.

**Approval gate.** Lock the outcome, lag, cell exclusions, weighting and inference protocol before estimating. Document thresholds and outcome alternatives with the same care as the main specification. [Model selection](model_selection.md)

Estimer, après approbation, si un IHH hors États-Unis plus élevé est associé à une variation annuelle ultérieure plus forte des exportations nominales.
