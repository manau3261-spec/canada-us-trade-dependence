# Design B · U.S. trade shocks and Canadian employment

**Status.** Feasibility of the employment outcome is verified at broad province–industry grain. A defensible tariff dose at the same grain and the requirements for causal inference are open.

1. **Research question.** Did employment move differently after the 2025 U.S. trade measures in Canadian province–industry cells that initially supplied more covered U.S.-bound exports?
2. **Mechanism.** A fall in demand or margins can reduce shifts, overtime, hiring or headcount in exporting industries. Suppliers, transportation and construction can respond indirectly and with different lags. Policy uncertainty and Canadian countermeasures also affect employment.
3. **Hypothesis.** Employment growth or the employment level weakens over time in directly exposed cells relative to less exposed cells. Examine hours as a separate early-response outcome only if an official compatible series and coverage are verified.
4. **Dependent variable.** Log employment in persons per month from the Labour Force Survey (LFS), using the published unadjusted estimates and modeling seasonality explicitly. Report survey suppression and data-quality flags. LFS measures employment by residence and industry of work, while CIMT identifies province of production; cross-border provincial commuting and other residence/workplace differences can attenuate the relationship.
5. **Main explanatory variables.** Construct a predetermined industry and province exposure using 2022–2024 Canadian U.S.-bound domestic export values and an audited product-to-producing-industry crosswalk, normalized by that cell’s pre-policy employment in persons. Multiply each implicated product’s baseline value by its verified statutory rate change for the **specific** policy and sum. The unit is baseline CAD exports times the tariff-rate change per worker. Use baseline exports and rates frozen before the relevant measure or separately date each effective change. Quantities, U.S. content, preference claims and partial code matches that cannot be audited go to a separate unresolved share; the pilot must state what the dose covers. A transportation or construction shock requires a verified customer/supply linkage, otherwise these are comparison or spillover outcomes.
6. **Controls.** Province–month absorbs local common demand, provincial policy and local currency effects. Industry–month absorbs national industry cycles, product prices and sector demand common across provinces. Use pre-period electricity or energy intensity, export composition, and clean countermeasure exposure only where sourced and identified. Do not condition on post-policy exports, migration or plant closures in the main specification.
7. **Unit.** Province × broad NAICS industry × calendar month. The main diagnostic compares manufacturing (31–33), construction (23), and transportation and warehousing (48–49). The comparison industries may absorb indirect spillovers. Any move to narrower LFS categories is conditional on verified coverage, compatible vintages and enough independent exposure.
8. **Window.** Ten provinces, January 2022–September 2026 as the first jointly verified calendar window. Exclude Canada’s aggregate and the territories from the LFS regression universe. Start with a shorter 2025 diagnostic around the legally cleanest date, expand only after separately coding legal regime changes and partial transition months. Freeze exposure weights before 2025.
9. **Sources.** Statistics Canada table 14-10-0022-01 LFS by industry and province. CIMT domestic exports at Canadian HS8; acquire and verify baseline years 2022–2023 and reuse verified 2024. Use the tariff and HTS annexes identified in the policy audit. A documented allocation from HS to producing industry is required. Canadian Supply and Use tables can inform product–industry allocation only after the link to HS/NAPCS and the required resolution are shown. An official concordance from HS to output categories alone does not assign producer industry. [LFS](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/cv.action?pid=1410002201) · [CIMT](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/2909a648-5753-4924-878a-b069392d9cde) · [Canadian supply and use tables](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/en/catalogue/15-602-X) · [Phase 3 coverage](../data/phase3/panel_coverage.csv) · [Policy audit](tariff_policy_timeline.md)
10. **Equation.** Let `e[g,i,m]` be employment, `D[g,i]` the frozen dose for one auditable shock, and `R_k[m]` indicators for pre-registered event-time bins. Specify `log e[g,i,m] = a[g,i] + b[i,m] + c[g,m] + s[g,i,month_of_year(m)] + sum_k beta[k] * D[g,i] * R_k[m] + u[g,i,m]`. Choose January 2025 as the reference for a March 2025 event. Label or omit February anticipation and March transition. Exact bin widths and horizon must follow the chosen event and feasible variation. With repeated measures, the single-shock coefficients describe the period if other policies cannot be isolated.
11. **Fixed effects.** Province–industry, industry–calendar-month, province–calendar-month and province–industry season-of-year, with redundant columns absorbed. Check rank after residualizing the dose. Cells cannot identify a differential response if dose differences disappear under industry and province controls.
12. **Inference.** Cluster by province to allow correlation across industries and months. Use ten-cluster restricted wild bootstrap with Webb weights as a sensitivity, report leverage and leave-one-province-out ranges. Common policy shocks across provinces make product-shock independence particularly important. Only propose shock-level shift-share inference after validating independent shocks and exogeneity. Use intervals to convey precision, especially with one policy cohort and effectively few positive-exposure provinces. [Shift-share identification, Borusyak, Hull and Jaravel](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24997)
13. **Identification assumptions.** Conditional differential trends would have been parallel without the chosen tariff changes. The baseline allocation and rates measure plausible geographic differences, and simultaneous events have no residual exposure-correlated effect. Large supplier, worker or cross-province spillovers undermine the comparison. Predetermined shares reduce mechanical reverse causality with employment during the event. The legal selection of product groups and the sensitivity of prior specialization to trends remain open.
14. **Confounding.** Canadian retaliation, product price cycles, U.S. demand shifts, monetary conditions, inventories, election-related uncertainty, plants already scheduled to close and provincial industrial policies. A steel-specific sample may be dominated by one manufacturing province and a few plants. Tariff eligibility and rules of origin may change differentially by input structure.
15. **Sample and missingness.** The planned grid has 10 × 3 × 57 = **1,710** possible employment observations. Phase 3 records full reported coverage of all three broad outcome groups in the relevant historical and recent windows, including all ten provinces. No number is certified for the tariff-linked sample. Suppressions increase at finer NAICS grain, series can be revised, and targeted legal exposures may span very few cells. Unmatched exposure stays unknown, with a minimum coverage threshold set before looking at estimates.
16. **Robustness.** Alternative pre-period weights, clean different-dose dates, pre-event trend checks, narrower high-coverage sectors and aggregation levels, employment levels and changes, alternative seasonal treatment, leave-one-province-out, direct exposure vs independently mapped spillover linkage, and a placebo date. Compare fixed baseline employment weighting to unweighted cell estimates and describe the corresponding target. Compare payroll outcomes only after separate provenance and coverage checks.
17. **Interpretation.** Descriptive and then associational until the dose and comparison meet the above conditions. An employment contrast would describe labour-market responses in the measured cells, with no direct conversion to national job losses, value added or welfare. In interdependent markets a direct dose contrast can incorporate displacement elsewhere.

**Go/no-go.** Require a documented product allocation, nontrivial within-industry regional dose variation, a useful overlap window and credible controls before promoting this design. [Model selection](model_selection.md)

## Synthèse en français

Relier l’emploi mensuel par province et groupe industriel à une exposition calculée à partir des exportations d’avant 2025. La faisabilité dépend de la concordance entre les codes de produits et l’industrie productrice, ainsi que du nombre de provinces réellement exposées et comparables.
