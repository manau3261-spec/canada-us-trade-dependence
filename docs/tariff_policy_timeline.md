# U.S. tariff chronology for Canadian goods · 2025–2026

Verified through November 18, 2026 against signed measures, official entry guidance and the USITC archive. This is a research chronology of major measures. Product codes in named annexes and the contemporaneous HTSUS notes govern coverage. Heading examples identify areas for audit and confer no eligibility by themselves. Effective dates refer to U.S. entry for consumption or withdrawal for consumption unless indicated. “Duty-free under CUSMA” refers to the treatment on entry, with the correct claim. U.S. documents use “USMCA” for CUSMA. The trade observations verified for joint recent analysis end in September 2026. ## Coding and measuring the shock

1. **Gross statutory schedule.** Build a dated register with legal event ID and authority, announcement and entry dates/times, HTS edition, ordinary HTS8/10, required Chapter 99 codes, origin, CUSMA and end-use conditions, basis of assessment, exclusions, quotas, date-specific stacking and ban flags. Keep the legal PDFs, HTS snapshots, checksums and identifiable source references. The USITC archive lists revisions through **2026 Revision 23, November 10**. Intermediate entry guidance may change the interpretation of a snapshot. [HTS archive](https://hts.usitc.gov/download/archive)
2. **Feasible comparison for Design A.** Audit a small March 12, 2025 full-value core-steel set. Match US HTS8/10 to Canadian export HS8 only through a validated common six-digit parent and stable HS edition. Require homogeneous line coverage or a verified exact alignment, documentation of earlier exclusions, no material conflicting policy in comparison lines, and a meaningful denominator. Tag multi-rate, content, quota and firm-dependent entries as unresolved. Keep them out of the main eligibility contrast. Reject broad industry and broad chapter tariff proxies. No production-ready all-product tariff mapping was established.
3. **Observed border duty ratio.** Where USITC provides matching imports-for-consumption customs values and calculated duties, sum duties and divide by summed customs value for the same Canada-origin HTS group, entry month and trade regime. Units are USD/USD. Retrieve matching imports-for-consumption values, duty-rate provisions and preference fields at their available grains. Record the query and vintage. USITC describes calculated duties as estimated from applicable rates and claims. They generally include specified Section 232 and 301 duties and exclude antidumping and countervailing assessments. Verify completeness for each applicable Chapter 99 regime and vintage. Do not divide U.S. duties by Canadian export dollars or compare a current duty ratio to an unweighted mean of statutory rates. Differential preference take-up, gaps, exclusions, zero imports and changing product mix all change the ratio. [USITC calculated-duty definitions](https://dataweb.usitc.gov/trade/search/Import/HTS.) · [DataWeb Query API](https://www.usitc.gov/applications/dataweb/api/dataweb_query_api.html)
4. **Incidence limits.** A ratio can quantify estimated duties relative to imports where the fields are available. It cannot recover net payments after refunds, firm-level exemptions, non-U.S. or metal-content shares, legal bans, incidence on consumers or exporters, or the counterfactual duty on trade that never occurred. Phase 4A verified the official access method and legal structure. A matched duty extract and validated rate series remain open. Keep statutory assignment as the proposed treatment and use observed ratios to diagnose exposure, with missing or unresolved mappings explicitly recorded.

## Synthèse en français

Plusieurs autorités imposent des droits différents selon la date d’entrée, le code tarifaire américain, la préférence ACEUM, le contenu ou les exemptions applicables. Le volet tarifaire recommandé se limite à un petit ensemble de produits d’acier visés le 12 mars 2025, sous réserve de vérifier les codes, les exemptions et le groupe de comparaison. Les données canadiennes d’exportation ne suffisent pas à mesurer les droits acquittés ni leur répartition économique.
